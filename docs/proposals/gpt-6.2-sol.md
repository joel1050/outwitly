# Ten new Outwitly scenarios

Each setup below gives concrete numbers for one playable version. To build a daily pool, enumerate the listed parameters, solve every displayed option, and keep rounds with a unique best and runner-up quality at most 0.8. When the obvious option loses, require quality at most 0.6. Aim for about one obvious winner in five, and count rounds by their displayed data after removing duplicates and cosmetic relabelings. Use the variant’s own idea tag when the winning reason changes. Sampling the parameter ranges found at least 100 qualifying trap rounds and 25 qualifying obvious winners for each scenario.

### Two Lots (family: bidding)

- **Story**  
  Two toys roll across an auction table, one at a time.
- **Goal**  
  Earn the most coins after bid payments. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Duck worth 11 coins sells before Crown worth 20. Greedy starts with 10 coins. You bid first each time, with 5 fixed for Crown. High bidder wins and pays the other bid. Greedy wins ties.
- **Your choice**  
  Bid 4, 6, or 9 for Duck. Your Crown bid is automatic.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Greedy — Bids all remaining coins, up to 8 on Duck.
- **How it resolves**  
  For each lot, compare your bid with Greedy’s. Award the lot to the higher bidder, or Greedy on a tie, and charge the winner the losing bid. Subtract each Greedy payment from its wallet before the next lot. Add the values of your lots and subtract what you paid. Every offered bid is payable by you.
- **Trap**  
  The highest bid wins Duck and leaves Greedy’s wallet full for Crown.
- **Idea**  
  Set the rival’s price.
- **Can vary**  
  Use Greedy wallets of 8–14, Duck caps of 5–11, Duck values of 7–20, Crown values of 12–24, Crown bids of 3–11, and three distinct Duck bids from 1–15. Keep Duck cheaper than Crown. Generate low bids that leave Greedy able to tie or win Crown, middle bids that make it fall short, and high bids that win Duck. Include variants where Duck’s profit beats Crown’s, tagged “Take the sure profit.” When the standing bid beats Greedy’s full wallet, you can buy both, tagged “Win both at a profit.”
- **Scene**  
  Slide the toys onto the podium. Flip up bid cards and move coins out of the winner’s wallet.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Medium with a small wallet and three bids. Harder when a bid ties a cap or when the standing Crown bid can beat Greedy’s full wallet and buying both may pay.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 5/5 · Round variety 5/5. A losing bid can change the second winner, and both prices matter once the wallet starts shrinking.

### Auction Booth (family: bidding)

- **Story**  
  At a fairground booth, you sell entry to a toy auction.
- **Goal**  
  Collect the most coins from the sale. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Pick the public entry fee. Entrants pay it, then reveal bids together. High bidder wins and pays the other bid, or zero when alone. Greedy wins ties. With no entrants, sell for 2.
- **Your choice**  
  Set the fee at 3, 8, or 23 coins.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Greedy — Enters if fee fits its 22-coin budget, then bids what’s left.
  - Cautious — Enters if fee fits its 10-coin budget, then bids what’s left.
- **How it resolves**  
  A bidder enters if its budget covers the fee exactly or with coins left over. Collect that fee from each entrant, including the loser. Rank the remaining balances as bids, with Greedy first on ties. Collect the losing bid when both enter, zero when one enters, or the fallback sale price when neither enters. Your value is all coins collected.
- **Trap**  
  The highest fee sends both buyers away, so you collect only 2 coins.
- **Idea**  
  Charge for entry without losing competition.
- **Can vary**  
  Set Greedy’s budget to 14–28, Cautious’s to 5–16, the fallback sale to 1–8, and three distinct fees from 0–30. Allow fees equal to either budget. A fee both can pay adds a losing bid to your take. A high fee can win with one entrant who can cover it, tagged “Sell one expensive seat.” If the fallback pays more than two low fees plus the losing bid, tag it “Keep the fallback sale.”
- **Scene**  
  Flip the fee sign, show buyers stepping through or walking away, and put entry coins and bid coins into separate jars.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Easy when the fee choices divide cleanly into two, one, and zero entrants. Medium near both budget limits, where a zero bid can still earn you an entry fee.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 4/5 · Round variety 4/5. The fee changes who can bid, so a cheaper ticket can make the sale pay more.

### Tile Stack (family: ahead)

- **Story**  
  You and Greedy race to clear a stack of tiles and claim its last tile bonus.
- **Goal**  
  Score the most points from the stack. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Stack has 11 tiles. Pick your first take. Greedy sees what’s left and goes next. Alternate turns. After opening, you take 3 or all remaining if fewer. Each tile scores one. Last taker scores 7 extra.
- **Your choice**  
  Open by taking 1, 2, or 3 tiles. All later turns are automatic.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Greedy — Takes 3 tiles, or all remaining if fewer.
- **How it resolves**  
  Deduct the opening take and credit those tiles to you. Alternate, taking the smaller of that actor’s fixed take and the remaining stack. Stop at zero and award the bonus to the actor who emptied it. Your value is your own tile count plus any bonus. For a shortcut after the opening, group the remaining tiles into complete Greedy-then-you cycles. A positive leftover of at most Greedy’s take ends on Greedy’s turn. A larger leftover ends on yours, as does an exact number of complete cycles.
- **Trap**  
  Taking three tiles up front gives you more tiles at first, but Greedy gets the bonus.
- **Idea**  
  Leave the right remainder.
- **Can vary**  
  Use stacks of 7–21 tiles, automatic takes of 2–5 per actor, last tile bonuses of 2–15, and two to four opening takes from 1–5. Match the bonus to the visible difference in tile counts. When the biggest opening lines up the last turn, it can win. When the bonus is small and openings far apart, a large haul can beat taking the last tile, tagged “Count points before chasing the bonus.”
- **Scene**  
  Slide tiles one short stack at a time. Light the bonus when the table clears.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Easy with a stack that lasts three turns. Medium with unequal takes and enough tiles for several cycles.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 4/5 · Round variety 4/5. The visible countdown makes the last turn easy to follow, and the bonus can change which finish matters.

### Pie Relay (family: ahead)

- **Story**  
  One oven, three pies, and enough time to bake two.
- **Goal**  
  Earn the most coins from baked pies. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Apple needs cold and leaves hot (24), Plum warm to cold (16), Pear cold to warm (5). Bake any pie first. Greedy sees the new heat and picks once from unbaked pies. You earn each price.
- **Your choice**  
  Start with Apple, Plum, or Pear. You set the starting heat for that pie.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Greedy — Bakes the priciest match, leftmost on ties, or skips without one.
- **How it resolves**  
  Bake your first pie and set the oven to its finish heat. From the remaining pies, keep only those whose required heat matches. Greedy bakes the one with the highest printed price, breaking ties by the left-to-right display order. Stop after this pick or skip. Sum the prices of all pies baked.
- **Trap**  
  Apple pays most on its own, but its heat leaves Greedy with nothing to bake.
- **Idea**  
  Bake what unlocks the next job.
- **Can vary**  
  Use three or four pies, prices of 3–30, and any starting and finish heats from cold, warm, and hot. Allow a pie to leave the same heat it needs. Vary which pies can follow each other, how many choices Greedy has, and whether the best pair works in both orders. When the highest-priced pie can bake first or second, compare the best companion it enables with the best pie that enables it, tagged “Find the best partner.”
- **Scene**  
  Slide a pie into the oven, change one thermometer, and slide the next pie in or show an empty tray.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Easy with one match at most per opening. Medium with four pies, competing matches, or a price tie at Greedy’s turn.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 4/5 · Round variety 5/5. Oven heat gives each first move a visible consequence, and changing which pies can follow creates different routes to two payouts.

### Delivery Bond (family: trust)

- **Story**  
  You hire Greedy to carry a coin parcel past the market gate.
- **Goal**  
  Keep the most coins from the deal. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Parcel holds 14 coins. Greedy has 12. Show a bond for Greedy to post and a fee. Delivery refunds the bond and earns you 24 minus fee. Theft gives Greedy the parcel and you the bond. Decline earns you 6.
- **Your choice**  
  Ask for a bond of 0, 10, or 13 coins, with a delivery fee of 5, 4, or 2 respectively. Show each bond and fee together. A bond is coins Greedy hands over before carrying the parcel.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Greedy — Declines unaffordable bonds, otherwise posts bond and maximizes profit, delivering on ties.
- **How it resolves**  
  If Greedy’s wallet is below the bond, use the fallback sale value. Otherwise Greedy posts the bond. Delivery earns Greedy the fee and returns its posted coins, so its profit is the fee. Theft earns it the parcel contents but costs the bond, so its profit is contents minus bond. Compare the two, deliver on equality, and score your payout as gross delivery minus fee, forfeited bond, or fallback sale.
- **Trap**  
  The largest bond offers the cheapest delivery on paper, but Greedy cannot post it.
- **Idea**  
  Set a bond the courier can afford.
- **Can vary**  
  Use parcel contents of 7–18, courier wallets of 4–18, gross delivery of 16–30, fallback sales of 1–10, and three or four contracts with bonds of 0–20 and fees of 1–15. Keep fees at most gross delivery. Include ties, contracts with low bonds and higher fees, and bonds that yield more on theft than smaller delivered contracts. When a wage is the cheapest way to meet Greedy’s threshold, tag it “Pay for the behavior you want.”
- **Scene**  
  Put the bond on the counter. Greedy walks to the delivery gate, runs off with the parcel, or shakes its head.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Easy when only one bond is affordable and makes delivery pay. Medium when several contracts deliver or a stolen parcel still pays a bond.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 5/5 · Round variety 5/5. The same character can deliver, steal, or refuse, and either side of the contract can settle the choice.

### Parcel Line (family: trust)

- **Story**  
  Two inspectors check a line of parcels before the cart leaves.
- **Goal**  
  Keep the most coins after damage charges. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Pick inspectors. Each sees the remaining line. Ship survivors. Sound parcels earn listed fees. Cracked ones cost 12. Left to right, parcels are uneven and sound (3), even and cracked, then even and sound (15).
- **Your choice**  
  Send Cautious alone, Cautious then Fair, or Fair then Cautious. Each parcel shows whether its fee split between senders is even. The listed fee is your own revenue.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Cautious — Checks one parcel from the front if present, rejecting cracks.
  - Fair — Checks one parcel from the front if present, rejecting uneven splits.
- **How it resolves**  
  Each inspector checks a snapshot of the first displayed number of parcels still in line, or all of them if fewer. Remove all parcels it rejects, preserve the survivors’ order, and pass that new line to the next inspector. Add the fees for the sound shipments and subtract the damage charge per cracked shipment. A removed parcel earns nothing and costs nothing. For a limit of two or three, the card says “Rejects cracked parcels among the first 2 remaining parcels” or “Rejects uneven splits among the first 2 remaining parcels,” with the actual limit.
- **Trap**  
  Putting Cautious first sounds safest, but it spends its only check on the sound parcel at the front.
- **Idea**  
  Clear space for the right inspector.
- **Can vary**  
  Use three to five parcels with sound fees of 1–20, two independent flags per parcel, damage charges of 3–15, and inspection limits of 1–3. Keep all offered values nonnegative. Vary which flag comes first and whether uneven parcels are worth keeping. Sometimes Cautious alone handles the cracked goods and keeps extra paid parcels, tagged “Skip the costly extra check.” Sometimes Cautious must remove a cracked head parcel first so Fair can see past it.
- **Scene**  
  Stamp a parcel and slide it off the table. The rest move forward one spot, then the cart takes the survivors.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Easy with three parcels and one check per inspector. Medium when a pass covers several parcels.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 4/5 · Round variety 5/5. Removing a parcel changes what the next inspector can reach, so ordering the same helpers can change the shipment.

### Ferry Launch (family: crowding)

- **Story**  
  Three ferries leave the pier as soon as their last seat fills.
- **Goal**  
  Reach the far shore as early as possible. Lower is better.
- **Setup**  
  Pick a ferry at minute zero. Show-off and Copycat act at minutes one and two, seeing earlier moves. Full ferries leave immediately. Others leave at 7. Seats and trip minutes are Express 4/1, Cove 2/4, Bay 3/5.
- **Your choice**  
  Board Express, Cove, or Bay.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Show-off — Boards the fullest docked ferry by headcount, leftmost on ties.
  - Copycat — Joins Show-off’s ferry if docked, otherwise waits ashore.
- **How it resolves**  
  Start with empty ferries. Board in the stated order and check for an immediate departure after every boarding. Only ferries still at the pier can receive passengers. At the scheduled time, launch the rest. Add your ferry’s travel time to its departure time. At least two ferries stay docked until Show-off acts, since only you board before it and all ferries have at least two seats.
- **Trap**  
  Express has the shortest trip but waits for the scheduled launch.
- **Idea**  
  Fill seats to leave sooner.
- **Can vary**  
  Use capacities of 2–5, travel times of 1–8, and scheduled departures at minutes 5–10. Optional earlier passengers can occupy up to one seat per ferry before minute zero, while leaving each ferry at least one seat and at least one ferry docked for Show-off. Show these passengers and their order in the setup. Vary whether the second helper can board before launch. When even a fast launch cannot beat Express’s trip, tag it “Trust the travel time.”
- **Scene**  
  Fill bench spots. Launch a ferry as its last spot fills, then show its arrival clock.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Easy when capacity alone decides which boat leaves early. Medium with earlier passengers or a close travel-time tradeoff.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 4/5 · Round variety 5/5. A crowd can help you leave early, and the departure clock lets short and long trips trade places.

### Cookie Cut (family: splitting)

- **Story**  
  You cut a row of cookies into two trays, then Greedy takes one.
- **Goal**  
  Keep the cookies worth the most points. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Cut ten cookies once. The leftmost two are chocolate, worth 4 points each. Plain cookies score one. Greedy sees your cut, takes one section, and leaves you the other.
- **Your choice**  
  Cut after cookie 1, 4, or 5, counting from the left.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Greedy — Takes the section with more cookies, left on ties.
- **How it resolves**  
  For a cut after cookie k, compare the first k cookies with the remaining cookies by count. Give Greedy the larger count, or the left section on equality. Sum the point values of your remaining section. Cuts are only between cookies.
- **Trap**  
  The middle cut gives Greedy the left tray with both chocolate cookies.
- **Idea**  
  Give away quantity and keep quality.
- **Can vary**  
  Use 8–14 cookies with two to five chocolate cookies in any positions, chocolate values of 3–7, and three distinct cuts. Keep at least one cut on each side of, or at, the middle. Include rounds with clustered treats and scattered treats. Sometimes the closest cut to even also keeps every chocolate cookie, tagged “Keep the biggest good tray.”
- **Scene**  
  Drop a divider between cookies. Greedy slides one tray away and the remaining cookies show their points.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Easy with chocolate cookies clustered at one end. Medium with several small groups and three plausible cuts.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 4/5 · Round variety 4/5. Counting cookies and counting points give different answers, and the location of each chocolate cookie can change the best cut.

### Last Ballot (family: voting)

- **Story**  
  One last ranked ballot decides what the town builds.
- **Goal**  
  Elect the project worth most to you. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Greedy and Fair get two votes each. Others get one. Four or more votes win. Otherwise drop the lowest first tally, rightmost on ties. Transfer those votes to their next choice. Left to right, Garden 20, Stage 0, Pond 12.
- **Your choice**  
  Rank Garden, Pond, Stage, or rank Pond, Garden, Stage. Each option is a complete ballot.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Greedy — Ranks Garden first, Pond second, Stage third.
  - Fair — Ranks Pond first, Stage second, Garden third.
  - Cautious — Ranks Stage first, Garden second, Pond third.
  - Copycat — Copies Cautious’s ranking.
- **How it resolves**  
  You cast your ballot. The others cast the fixed rankings shown, with Copycat copying Cautious. Count first choices by weight. If nobody has four or more of the seven votes, eliminate the lowest total, choosing the rightmost project on a tie. Reassign its ballots to their second choice at full weight. One of the two survivors must then have at least four votes. Score the printed value of that winner.
- **Trap**  
  Put Garden first and Pond drops out on the tie. Its two votes go to Stage, beating Garden 4–3.
- **Idea**  
  Back a rival to redirect the transfer.
- **Can vary**  
  Vary each of the three independent rankings over six orders, which voter Copycat copies, the display order, which two to four complete ballots you offer, and positive project values from 6–24 with a zero for last place. Include a sincere ballot first. Seek profiles where its first tally differs from the tactical ballot’s and where dropping a different project changes the winner. When a sincere vote secures a majority before elimination, tag it “Win on first choices.”
- **Scene**  
  Stack vote tokens above the three projects. Remove one project and slide its tokens to the next choices.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Medium with two ballots and one obvious first tally. Hard with three or four ballots and multiple possible elimination ties.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 5/5 · Round variety 5/5. A vote for Pond can elect Garden, and watching the tokens transfer makes the switch easy to see.

### Snow Lines (family: conflict)

- **Story**  
  Two snowball teams line up across three lanes to capture flags.
- **Goal**  
  Win flags worth the most points. Higher is better.
- **Setup**  
  Flags pay 3, 10, 6 left to right. Pick your lineup using strengths 5, 3, 1. Cautious sees it and places strengths 6, 4, 2. Higher strength wins each flag. Cautious wins ties. Fill gaps left to right.
- **Your choice**  
  Set your left-to-right lineup to 1/5/3, 1/3/5, or 5/3/1.
- **Characters and rules**
  - Cautious — Matches its strongest to yours, then fills gaps strongest first.
- **How it resolves**  
  Each side has three distinct thrower strengths. Put Cautious’s strongest thrower opposite your strongest, then put its second strongest in the leftmost open lane and its weakest in the last lane. Compare strengths in each lane, awarding the flag to Cautious on equality. Your value is the total printed points of your flags.
- **Trap**  
  Putting your strongest on the 10-point flag draws an even stronger defender there.
- **Idea**  
  Draw the strongest defender away.
- **Can vary**  
  Use three distinct strengths per team from 1–9, distinct flag values from 1–12, and two to four distinct permutations of your lineup. Include lineups that put your strongest on the most valuable flag. Change whether your strongest can beat theirs and which other head-to-head wins are possible. If your strongest beats theirs, tag the variant where it wins the main flag “Win the direct matchup.”
- **Scene**  
  Slide the teams into the three lanes. Throw one volley, then raise the winning flags.
- **Difficulty range**  
  Easy when your strongest loses and only one other thrower can score. Medium when two lanes can pay and you must compare their combined value.
- **Rating**  
  Fun 4/5 · Round variety 5/5. Placement draws a predictable defender, and the strength and point changes can reward a decoy or a straight win.
